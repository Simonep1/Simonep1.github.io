Statistical Independence

Contingency tables, conditional frequencies and Pearson's chi-square test.

1. Independence

In probability, X and F are independent if their joint probability equals the product of their marginal probabilities for every pair of values.

P(X = x, F = f) = P(X = x) · P(F = f)

Equivalently, P(F = f | X = x) = P(F = f), whenever P(X = x) > 0.

In statistics, independence is checked using a finite sample. Even if the variables are independent in the population, sample frequencies may differ because of chance. We use the chi-square test to evaluate these differences.

5. Chi-square test

    
The chi-square statistic compares the observed counts O with the expected counts E:

    
χ² = Σ (Oij − Eij)² / Eij

6. Conclusion

    

    
Statistical association does not prove causation. Different penguin species were observed on different islands, but this test cannot tell us why they are distributed that way. Habitat, sampling and other factors may be involved.
